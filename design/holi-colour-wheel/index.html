Holi colour wheel rangoli

Eight slices in eight colours: the quickest way to use every packet of gulal left after Holi. Children can fill one slice each, which makes it a good first group rangoli.

Level
Easy
Size
About 30 to 45 cm across (1 to 1.5 feet)
Time
About 15 to 20 minutes
Steps
4
Good for
Holi, children, beginners

Colours you need

RedOrangeYellowLight greenTealBluePurplePinkWhite

Tap Outline to see the design as lines, then print it to practise on paper or to colour in.

How to draw it, step by step

Sweep the floor and wipe it with a damp cloth so the powder grips. Mark the centre with chalk. Then follow the pictures. Each one adds one part.

  1. Draw a large circle and divide it into eight equal slices, like cutting a cake. Fill each slice with a different colour.

  2. Cover the centre, where the slices meet, with a neat white circle.

  3. Place eight white dots in a ring, one in each gap. Drop a pinch of powder and press lightly for each dot.

  4. Draw a thin white circle around the design. A string held at the centre, with chalk at the other end, keeps it round.

Tip: Put colours that contrast next to each other, such as yellow beside purple, so that each slice stands out.

Make it your own

  • Change the colours to what you have at home. Any two strong colours and white are enough.
  • Make it smaller for a puja thali or a table, or repeat it three times in a row along a wall.
  • Print the outline and let children colour it first. It is good practice before the powder comes out.
