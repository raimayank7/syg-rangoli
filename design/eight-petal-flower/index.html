Eight petal flower rangoli

The first rangoli most people learn: one flower with eight petals, a ring of dots and a lace edge. It teaches the two skills every bigger design needs, which are dividing a circle into equal parts and keeping petals the same size.

Level
Easy
Size
About 30 to 45 cm across (1 to 1.5 feet)
Time
About 15 to 20 minutes
Steps
5
Good for
Every day, beginners, Diwali

Colours you need

YellowPinkWhiteOrange

Tap Outline to see the design as lines, then print it to practise on paper or to colour in.

How to draw it, step by step

Sweep the floor and wipe it with a damp cloth so the powder grips. Mark the centre with chalk. Then follow the pictures. Each one adds one part.

  1. Make a small filled circle in yellow. This is the centre of the whole design, so place it carefully.

  2. Draw eight long pointed petals around the centre, filled in pink. Mark the eight tips with dots first so the petals come out even.

  3. Place eight white dots in a ring, one in each gap. Drop a pinch of powder and press lightly for each dot.

  4. Draw a thin white circle around the design. A string held at the centre, with chalk at the other end, keeps it round.

  5. Finish the edge with sixteen small half-circles in orange, touching each other like a lace border.

Tip: To get eight equal parts, mark a cross first (top, bottom, left, right), then one mark halfway between each pair.

Where it works best

It fits almost anywhere: a doorstep, the middle of a small room, the floor in front of the puja shelf. Because it is small and round, it also works as the centre of something larger. Draw it first, then add a border or a second ring of petals if time allows.

What usually goes wrong, and the fix

The usual fault is petals of different widths. That comes from skipping the eight marks and starting to draw at once. The second is dots that drift inward or outward: place them on a chalk circle drawn with string, one dot opposite each petal tip and one between. More fixes are on rangoli problems and fixes.

Make it your own

  • Change the colours to what you have at home. Any two strong colours and white are enough.
  • Make it smaller for a puja thali or a table, or repeat it three times in a row along a wall.
  • Print the outline and let children colour it first. It is good practice before the powder comes out.
