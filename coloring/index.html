How it works

  1. Choose a picture. Each one is marked easy, medium or more detail.
  2. Print it, with the "Print the blank page" button. It fits A4 paper upright and has a line for the child's name.
  3. Or colour it on the screen. Tap a colour, tap a part of the picture. This is handy in a waiting room or on a journey, and it wastes no paper.
  4. Talk while you colour. Every page has colour ideas, a few true facts to read aloud and two questions to ask.

Why colouring is worth the time

Colouring looks like play, and it is. It is also quiet practice for the small muscles of the hand that a child will soon need for writing. Holding a crayon, pressing hard or softly, stopping at a line, choosing which colour goes where: each is a skill. A page finished from edge to edge teaches a child to stay with one task, and a page proudly shown to a grandparent teaches something about pride in work. None of this needs to be said to the child. Put the page and the crayons on the table and sit nearby.

There is more in the guides for parents: what to expect at each age, crayons, pencils or paints, colour names in Hindi and English with simple colour mixing, and things to make from a finished page.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. There is nothing to pay and no account. Advertisements on the guide pages pay for the site; the picture pages have none.

Can I print these for my class or tuition group?

Yes, as many copies as you need. Please do not sell the pages or post the whole set elsewhere as your own.

Is my child's colouring saved?

No. It stays on the screen until the page is closed. To keep it, use "Print my colours" and choose "Save as PDF" in the print window, or take a screenshot.

The picture prints small or is cut off. What should I do?

Choose A4 and "Portrait" in the print window, set the scale to "Fit to page" or 100%, and turn off headers and footers. See how to print colouring pages.
