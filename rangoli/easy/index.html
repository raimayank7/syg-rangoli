Easy rangoli designs for beginners

Every design on this page can be finished in about twenty minutes by someone who has never made a rangoli. They use large shapes, few colours and no fine lines.

A one-week practice plan

  1. Day 1: on paper, draw circles and divide them into four, then eight parts.
  2. Day 2: on paper, draw petals from the centre to each mark until they look alike.
  3. Day 3: on the floor, practise letting chalk powder or rice flour fall in a thin line from your fingers.
  4. Day 4: draw the white line flower, lines only.
  5. Day 5: draw the eight petal flower with two colours.
  6. Day 6: add a border to it.
  7. Day 7: try a dot design.

Rangoli with children

  • Draw the outline yourself and let children fill the colours.
  • Give each child one colour and one spoon or small bottle.
  • Flower petals, coloured rice and pulses are easier for small hands than fine powder, and easier to clean up.
  • Print the outline of a design for colouring while the floor is being prepared.

Questions people ask

Which is the easiest rangoli?

A flower with eight petals and a ring of dots. It needs two colours and teaches the basic shapes used in larger designs.

Can I use chalk instead of powder?

Yes. Chalk on a dry floor is a good way to practise and to sketch the design before adding powder. Wet chalk gives a brighter, longer-lasting line.

What can small children use instead of powder?

Flower petals, coloured rice, pulses or torn coloured paper. They are easy to hold and safe to handle.
