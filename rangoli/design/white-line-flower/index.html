White line flower rangoli

A design in white lines only, the way a daily threshold rangoli is drawn with rice flour or chalk powder. No filling is needed, so it takes a few minutes once your hand knows the shape.

Level
Easy
Size
About 30 to 45 cm across (1 to 1.5 feet)
Time
About 15 to 20 minutes
Steps
6
Good for
Every day, beginners

Colours you need

White

Tap Outline to see the design as lines, then print it to practise on paper or to colour in.

How to draw it, step by step

Sweep the floor and wipe it with a damp cloth so the powder grips. Mark the centre with chalk. Then follow the pictures. Each one adds one part.

  1. Draw a thin white circle around the design. A string held at the centre, with chalk at the other end, keeps it round.

  2. Draw eight long pointed petals around the centre, as outlines in white. Mark the eight tips with dots first so the petals come out even.

  3. Draw eight long pointed petals in the gaps between the earlier ones, as outlines in white. Mark the eight tips with dots first so the petals come out even.

  4. Place eight white dots in a ring. Drop a pinch of powder and press lightly for each dot.

  5. Draw a thin white circle around the design. A string held at the centre, with chalk at the other end, keeps it round.

  6. Place sixteen white dots in a ring, one in each gap. Drop a pinch of powder and press lightly for each dot.

Tip: Let the powder run in a thin, steady stream between your thumb and first finger, and move your whole arm, not just the wrist.

Where it works best

This is the everyday design for the threshold, drawn in the morning after the doorstep has been washed. It needs one material and a few minutes. On a red oxide or dark stone floor, a single white line design looks finer than any amount of colour.

What usually goes wrong, and the fix

Broken or wobbly lines come from a hand held too high and powder released in bursts. Keep your fingers a few centimetres from the floor, let the powder run steadily and move your arm from the shoulder. Practise the petal shape ten times on a spare patch of floor first. More fixes are on rangoli problems and fixes.

Make it your own

  • Change the colours to what you have at home. Any two strong colours and white are enough.
  • Make it smaller for a puja thali or a table, or repeat it three times in a row along a wall.
  • Print the outline and let children colour it first. It is good practice before the powder comes out.
