Rangoli

Peacock feathers rangoli

Ten green feathers fan out from a blue centre, each with the blue eye that makes a peacock feather. The peacock feather is linked with Lord Krishna, so the design is popular at Janmashtami.

Level
Medium
Size
About 60 cm across (2 feet)
Time
About 30 to 45 minutes
Steps
5
Good for
Navratri, Janmashtami

Colours you need

BlueGreenDark blueYellowTeal

Tap Outline to see the design as lines, then print it to practise on paper or to colour in.

How to draw it, step by step

Sweep the floor and wipe it with a damp cloth so the powder grips. Mark the centre with chalk. Then follow the pictures. Each one adds one part.

  1. Make a small filled circle in blue. This is the centre of the whole design, so place it carefully.

  2. Draw ten long pointed petals around the centre, filled in green. Mark the ten tips with dots first so the petals come out even.

  3. Near the tip of each petal add an oval "eye" in blue with a dark blue dot inside, like a peacock feather.

  4. Place ten yellow dots in a ring, one in each gap. Drop a pinch of powder and press lightly for each dot.

  5. Draw a thin teal circle around the design. A string held at the centre, with chalk at the other end, keeps it round.

Tip: Make the petals long and narrow. The feather look comes from the slim shape and the eye near the tip.

Where it works best

For Janmashtami, place it near the jhula or the decorated corner for Krishna. During Navratri it suits the entrance. The blues and greens are cooler than most festival rangoli, so it stands out among the reds and yellows of a society competition.

What usually goes wrong, and the fix

Ten parts are the hard thing here. Mark five points by eye, adjust until they are even, then halve each gap. Put the dark blue eye of each feather well out towards the tip. If the eyes sit near the centre, the design reads as a flower, not as feathers. More fixes are on rangoli problems and fixes.

Make it your own

  • Swap one ring of petals for flower petals or for real diyas placed on the floor.
  • Add one of the border designs around it to make it larger without drawing a harder centre.
  • Print the outline and try two or three colour plans with pencils before you choose.
