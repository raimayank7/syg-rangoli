Rangoli

Rangoli names across India

Decorating the floor at the entrance is one of the most widely shared customs in India, and almost every region has its own name, material and style for it. The idea is the same everywhere: a clean, decorated threshold welcomes guests and good fortune.

NameWhereWhat is special
RangoliMaharashtra, Karnataka, Gujarat and much of north IndiaColoured powders, often in round designs filled with colour.
KolamTamil NaduLines of white rice flour drawn around a grid of dots, renewed every morning.
Muggu (muggulu)Andhra Pradesh and TelanganaClose to kolam. Sankranti is its great season.
PookalamKeralaA carpet of flower petals, laid during Onam.
Alpana (alpona)West Bengal and AssamPainted with the fingers in white rice paste for Lakshmi Puja and other festivals.
AripanBihar, especially MithilaRice paste designs drawn for pujas and family ceremonies.
MandanaRajasthan and Madhya PradeshWhite chalk or lime patterns on floors and walls plastered with red clay.
Chowk puranaUttar Pradesh, Punjab, Haryana and ChhattisgarhSquare designs in flour, drawn where a puja or a wedding ritual will take place.
Jhoti or chitaOdishaWhite rice paste motifs on floors and walls, especially for Lakshmi Puja.
AipanUttarakhand (Kumaon)White rice paste on a red clay base, at doorsteps and puja places.
SathiyaGujaratAuspicious designs drawn at the threshold, renewed for festivals.

Powder, paste or petals

The styles fall into three groups by material. Dry powder, as in rangoli, kolam and muggu, is drawn fresh and swept away. Wet rice paste, as in alpana, aripan, jhoti and aipan, is painted on and dries into a white design that lasts for days. Flowers, as in the pookalam, are laid for a single day.

What they share

  • They are drawn at the threshold, in the courtyard or at the place of worship.
  • They are renewed: daily in some regions, for each festival in others.
  • The designs are passed down within families, usually from mothers and grandmothers.
  • Most are symmetrical and are built outward from a centre or from a grid.

Customs and names vary from district to district and from family to family, so you may know a name or a detail that differs from this short list.
